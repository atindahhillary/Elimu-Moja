SELution

Learning through play, made regional.

A play-based programme that delivers four connected strands, social and emotional learning, foundational literacy and numeracy, unplugged coding, and robotics, through one teacher-training pathway and one measurement spine. Designed for low-resource East African classrooms and built directly on the priorities set at the 2nd EAC Regional Education Conference.

1. The opportunity

The 2nd EAC Regional Education Conference put foundational learning (FLN), social and emotional learning (SEL) and parental engagement at the centre of the regional agenda, and repeatedly named play as the pedagogy that delivers them. That consensus exists across at least four partner states. What does not exist is a concrete, comparable programme that carries it into classrooms.

SELution fills that gap. It is not a new policy priority to argue for; it is a delivery vehicle for priorities ministries have already adopted. The institutional context, the councils, frameworks and data systems this would sit under, is documented separately in the regional systems brief.

2. What SELution is

Four strands

  • SEL. Naming emotions, turn-taking, calming routines, friendship and conflict repair. The backbone for the other three.
  • Foundational literacy & numeracy. Letter sounds, decoding, number sense and early operations through games and manipulatives, mapped to the national curriculum.
  • Unplugged coding. Sequencing, loops and debugging with no device: floor grids, arrow cards, children as the “robot”.
  • Robotics. The same computational thinking with a physical build, using low-cost and recycled parts and one kit shared across a cluster of schools.

Play is the method

Every strand runs on one cycle: goal, game, roles, reflect, record. Each session has a clear learning target, a game with a rule, a role for every child, a short reflection, and a record the teacher completes in about ten seconds. That shared cycle is what makes four strands behave as one programme, and the record is the raw material for measurement.

A school can begin with SEL and foundational learning alone. Unplugged coding needs nothing but cards and chalk; robotics is added when a cluster is ready for a shared kit.

4. The pilot model

Unit
One district cluster, 8 to 15 schools.
Duration
One to two school terms, with baseline and endline assessment.
Delivered by
An implementing partner working inside the existing school system, not a parallel structure.
Teacher input
2 to 3 days of hands-on training, then fortnightly coaching visits.
Materials
Bottle caps, cards, chalk grids, recycled parts, one shared robotics kit per cluster.
Measurement
Per-child records roll up to a cluster dashboard; endline repeats the baseline.
Cost driver
Teacher coaching. Materials are deliberately cheap so spend concentrates on the part that changes outcomes.

Sequence: cluster and baseline → train teachers → run strands → engage parents → measure and report → joint decision on scale against a pre-agreed evidence threshold.

5. What we are seeking

Implementing partners (for example Teach for Kenya)

  • Host a first pilot in a cluster of your schools; provide teachers or fellows for training and coaching.
  • In return: a ready, regionally-aligned curriculum, all materials and the measurement dashboard, and a co-owned evidence base.

Country contacts (Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, South Sudan)

  • Name a district and a local implementer; open the door to the district education office; help localise take-home materials.
  • In return: a pilot on a shared regional model with comparable data, and first call on the trainer-of-trainers pathway.

Ministries and EAC bodies

  • A letter of support, a curriculum officer to confirm the FLN mapping, and a seat at the endline review.
  • In return: demonstrable delivery against FLN, SEL and digital-skills commitments, evidence generated on your own curriculum, no pilot-stage cost.

Funders

  • Fund one or two district-cluster pilots and the teacher-coaching layer; agree the evidence threshold for scale up front.
  • In return: four-country regional reach from one comparable pilot design, low unit cost, and a clean per-child before-and-after.
