# Advisory review 1: regional integration, teacher qualifications and mobility, scorecard precedents

> AI-assisted desk research prepared to inform the Elimu Moja concept. It surveys public sources on EAC and AU instruments and comparable regions. Treat every claim as a lead to verify against the primary source before it is used in policy. Links are inline.

---

Prepared for the advisory council following the 2nd EAC Regional Education Conference (Nairobi, 24 to 28 August 2026, "Transforming Education in East Africa: From Commitments to Impact"). The conference was convened by the EAC Secretariat with IUCEA, RELI Africa and partners, and centred on foundational learning, teacher capacity, inclusive education, future-ready skills, digitalisation, and financing and governance ([reliafrica.org](https://reliafrica.org/2nd-EAC-Education-Conference/), [africannewspage.net](https://www.africannewspage.net/2026/08/east-african-community-eac-to-convene-2nd-regional-education-conference-in-nairobi-to-drive-education-transformation/), [peopledaily.digital](https://peopledaily.digital/news/eac-steps-up-efforts-to-harmonise-education-systems-across-east-africa)).

Scope note: the EAC now has 8 partner states (Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, DR Congo since 2022, Somalia since 2024). Most existing harmonisation instruments were built for the original 5 to 6 Anglophone members and predate the accession of Francophone DRC and Somalia, which is itself a central design problem (see Section 4).

## 1. Existing EAC harmonisation instruments for education

### 1.1 EAC Treaty (1999), Chapter Eighteen "Co-operation in Social Sectors"
- Article 102 commits partner states to co-ordinate human resources development policies and to the **harmonisation of curricula, examination, certification and accreditation** of educational and training institutions through joint action, plus mutual recognition of academic qualifications and the exchange of teachers ([eac.int/education](https://www.eac.int/education), [theeastafrican.co.ke](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/news/east-africa/east-africa-education-systems-3829878)).
- Article 103 commits them to promote co-operation in science and technology (the legal basis later used to create EASTECO) ([easteco.org](https://easteco.org/about-easteco/)).
- Status: framework obligations, largely aspirational. They created the mandate but no enforcement mechanism, no timetable, and no dedicated financing line.

### 1.2 Common Market Protocol (2009, in force 1 July 2010), Article 11
- Article 11 requires partner states, for the free movement of labour, to **mutually recognise academic and professional qualifications, experience, licences and certifications** granted in other partner states, and to **harmonise curricula, examinations, standards, certification and accreditation** of institutions ([Common Market Protocol PDF](https://eacj.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/Common-Market-Protocol.pdf), [africanlii.org](https://africanlii.org/en/akn/aa-eac/act/protocol/2009/common-market-protocol/eng@2009-11-20)).
- Operationalised through the EAC Common Market (Mutual Recognition of Academic and Professional Qualifications) Regulations and profession-specific **Mutual Recognition Agreements (MRAs)**:
  - **Accountancy and auditors** signed 14 September 2011 ([icpak.com](https://www.icpak.com/eacia-mutual-recognition-agreement/)).
  - **Engineers** signed 7 December 2012; South Sudan acceded in 2024 ([eac.int](https://www.eac.int/press-releases/3184-the-republic-of-south-sudan-signs-mutual-recognition-agreement-for-engineers,-advancing-cross-border-mobility-and-regional-integration)).
  - **Architects** signed; **veterinary and para-veterinary professionals** signed March 2016 ([eac.int/education/mutual-recognition-agreements](https://www.eac.int/education/mutual-recognition-agreements)).
  - **Advocates and land surveyors**: still under negotiation.
- What has stalled: MRAs cover only a handful of elite professions; domestication into national licensing law is incomplete; work-permit and quota regimes persist in practice, so "free movement of labour" for teachers and most education workers is not yet real ([wenr.wes.org](https://wenr.wes.org/2018/12/common-higher-education-area-chea-of-the-east-african-community)).

### 1.3 Harmonised Curriculum Structures and Frameworks (drafted 2014)
- KICD-led drafting produced Draft Harmonised Curriculum Structures and Frameworks for primary, secondary, and secondary teacher education (diploma), dated August 2014, building on a process that began in 1998 ([primary draft](https://kicd.ac.ke/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/DraftHarmonisedCurriculumStructureandFrameworkforPrimaryEducation070814.pdf), [secondary draft](https://kicd.ac.ke/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/DraftHarmonisedCurriculumStructuresandFrameworkforSecondaryEducation070814.pdf)).
- What has stalled: never adopted as a common structure. Each state ran its own competency-based reform on its own timeline (Rwanda CBC 2015, Kenya CBC then CBE from 2017, Uganda lower-secondary 2020, Tanzania, Burundi), which **increased divergence** in structure, entry ages, cycle lengths and assessment. Structural mismatch remains: Kenya, Rwanda and South Sudan use a 4-year first degree after 12 years of school; Tanzania and Uganda use a 3-year degree after 13 years; Burundi 13 plus 4. Cross-border applicants often face bridging courses.

### 1.4 East African Qualifications Framework for Higher Education (EAQFHE) and the Common Higher Education Area
- **EAQFHE**: unveiled by IUCEA in 2015 as a generic instrument for anchoring national qualifications frameworks, codifying degree length, structure and learning outcomes across eight levels ([iucea.org](https://www.iucea.org/mdocs-posts/eaqffhe/), [ETF slide deck](https://www.etf.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2020-10/session_5_eaqfhe_6th_plw_22oct.pdf)).
- **EACATS (East African Credit Accumulation and Transfer System)**: 1 credit = 10 notional learning hours; 120 credits = one full-time year, mirroring ECTS ([wenr.wes.org](https://wenr.wes.org/2018/12/common-higher-education-area-chea-of-the-east-african-community)).
- **Common Higher Education Area (CHEA)**: declared by EAC Heads of State at the 18th Ordinary Summit, 20 May 2017, Dar es Salaam. Pillars: curriculum harmonisation, credit transfer, quality assurance, recognition of qualifications, and staff and student mobility ([iucea.org declaration](https://iucea.org/declaration-chea/)).
- What has stalled: EAQFHE is not implemented; EACATS is not adopted; the East African Graduation Statement is not issued; partner states resisted a binding regional accreditation agency; IUCEA relies on voluntary cooperation and covers a minority of the region's universities; chronic underfunding and protection of local students and jobs are repeatedly cited as blockers ([thepienews.com](https://thepienews.com/hurdles-ahead-east-african-community-common-higher-education-area/)).

### 1.5 EAC TVET Qualifications Framework and Harmonisation Strategy
- A Regional TVET Qualifications Framework was adopted by Ethiopia, Kenya and Tanzania under the World Bank EASTRIP project (declaration signed 24 May 2022); IUCEA convenes a Technical Working Group to bring in Burundi, Rwanda, South Sudan and Uganda ([acqf.africa](https://acqf.africa/news/east-africa-develops-regional-tvet-qualification-framework)).
- A draft EAC TVET Qualifications Framework covering all partner states went through a validation workshop in Dar es Salaam in August 2026; developed but **awaiting Sectoral Council approval** ([knqa.go.ke](https://knqa.go.ke/knqa-participates-in-the-validation-of-the-draft-east-africa-community-eac-tvet-qualifications-framework/)).

### 1.6 IUCEA, EASTECO, EAKC
- **IUCEA** (Kampala): statutory EAC institution steering higher-education harmonisation, quality assurance and mobility; membership voluntary; no enforcement power.
- **EASTECO** (Kigali): established 2007; coordinates regional science, technology and innovation policy.
- **East African Kiswahili Commission** (Zanzibar): promotes Kiswahili and runs a Mobility Programme. Kiswahili, English and French are EAC official languages following the amendment of Treaty Article 137 at the 24th Summit (2022), but there is no binding regional policy requiring Kiswahili in education; the Council has only "directed" partner states to draft national Kiswahili language policies ([eac.int](https://www.eac.int/press-releases/138-education,-science-technology-news/756-eac-partner-states-directed-to-formulate-national-kiswahili-language-policies)).

### 1.7 Sectoral Council on Education, Science and Technology, Culture and Sports (SCESTCS)
- The decision-making organ for the sector, reporting to the Council of Ministers; meets roughly annually. It oversees IUCEA, EASTECO and EAKC and approves strategies and roadmaps. Decisions are taken by **consensus of all partner states**, so any single ministry can slow or block harmonisation.

### 1.8 EAC education sector strategic plans
- The EAC has adopted seven sub-sector strategic plans (pre-primary, primary, secondary, teacher education, TVET, continuing and non-formal education, special needs education) under an Education Sector Development Plan ([standardmedia.co.ke](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/education/article/2001556360/eac-pushes-for-harmonised-education-systems-to-close-regional-learning-gaps)).
- Gap: there is **no common regional scorecard or monitoring instrument** attached to these plans, and no regional education statistical series.

### 1.9 Summary: on paper vs operationalised vs stalled

| Instrument | On paper | Operationalised | Stalled or not done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treaty Arts. 102 to 103 | Yes | Mandate only | No timetable, no enforcement |
| Common Market Art. 11 plus MRAs | Yes | 4 professions | Advocates and surveyors pending; weak domestication; teachers not covered |
| Harmonised curriculum frameworks (2014) | Drafted | No | Never adopted; national reforms diverged |
| EAQFHE, EACATS, Graduation Statement | Yes (2015) | No | Not implemented |
| CHEA declaration (2017) | Yes | Partial | No regional accreditation agency; low IUCEA coverage |
| TVET Qualifications Framework | Drafted; 3-country version 2022 | Partial | Full 8-state framework awaiting approval |
| Kiswahili as medium of instruction | Official language status (2022) | No | No binding education-language policy |
| Regional scorecard or EMIS | No | No | Does not exist |

## 2. Teacher qualification and mobility precedents to borrow from

### 2.1 African Union: CTQF, CFSCTP and the Continental Teacher Mobility Protocol
- The **Continental Teacher Qualification Framework (CTQF)** sets the classification and benchmarks of initial teacher education, continuing professional development, and the professional requirements for registration and licensing of teachers and school leaders across AU member states. It is designed as a "translation device" between different qualifications systems to enable comparability and mobility, aligned with UNESCO and ILO guidance ([Teacher Task Force CTQF PDF](https://teachertaskforce.org/sites/default/files/2020-09/Continental%20Teacher%20Qualification%20Framework_EN.pdf)).
- The **Continental Framework of Standards and Competences for the Teaching Profession (CFSCTP)** specifies the knowledge, skills and conduct expected of teachers and school leaders; the AU has approved it and asked member states to begin implementation.
- The **African Continental Teacher Mobility Protocol** was developed 2016 to 2018 by UNESCO IICBA and the AU Commission ([au.int](https://au.int/en/pressreleases/20180906/technical-consultation-au-continental-teacher-mobility-motivation-and)).
- Reality check: adoption and domestication remain limited; many states lack the capacity to align national frameworks with the CTQF. For the EAC this means the CTQF is a ready-made spine to adopt, not a solved problem to copy.

### 2.2 African Continental Qualifications Framework (ACQF) and micro-credentials
- The ACQF policy document was validated by AU member states in July 2023; ACQF-II (2023 to 2026, AUDA-NEPAD with AU and EU and ETF support) focuses on implementation, including recognition of prior learning, credit transfer, and capacity development ([acqf.africa](https://acqf.africa/about/overview)).
- The **ACQF Micro-credentials Handbook** gives Africa-specific definitions and quality principles for stackable short credentials and digital badges, and flags the need for a pan-African verification and portability layer ([acqf.africa micro-credentials](https://acqf.africa/resources/micro-credentials)).

### 2.3 SADC: qualifications framework plus teacher-specific instruments
- The **SADC Qualifications Framework (SADCQF)**: a 10-level regional reference translation device, with a Qualifications Recognition Manual and verification mechanisms ([sadc.int](https://www.sadc.int/latest-news/sadc-countries-advanced-stage-aligning-national-qualifications-frameworks-regional)).
- SADC has separately developed **SADC Teacher Standards and Competences** and a **SADC Regional Framework for Continuing Professional Development for Teachers**, explicitly to enable teacher mobility across national boundaries ([SADC CPD framework PDF](https://www.sadc.int/sites/default/files/2022-08/Regional%20CPD%20Framework%20for%20Teachers.pdf)). Design pattern: separate the credential framework from the professional standards framework from the CPD framework, and adopt all three.

### 2.4 EU Directive 2005/36/EC (as amended by 2013/55/EU)
- Three recognition regimes: automatic recognition for professions with harmonised minimum training (teachers deliberately not on that list); a general system with case-by-case comparison and a bounded compensation measure (an adaptation period or an aptitude test, not both) where "substantial differences" exist; and temporary provision of services with a prior declaration ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/36/oj/eng)).
- Enabling machinery worth copying: the **European Professional Card** (a standardised electronic procedure on the Internal Market Information System); **Common Training Frameworks**; and above all an **alert mechanism** requiring regulators to warn each other when a professional, including a teacher or anyone working with minors, is banned or restricted.

### 2.5 ASEAN: MRAs plus the AQRF
- ASEAN MRAs cover eight fields. Mechanics: a regional coordinating committee maintains a register; a professional in good standing at home registers as a "Registered Foreign Professional" who still works with a local professional and complies fully with host law. **MRAs do not override domestic licensing.**
- The **ASEAN Qualifications Reference Framework (AQRF)** is an 8-level voluntary common reference, with a deliberately neutral influence on national systems and country referencing reports validated by an AQRF Committee. Lesson: the neutral, voluntary, peer-referenced design got 10 diverse countries to participate; but ASEAN's MRAs are widely assessed as low-utilisation because licensing, language and quotas still bind.

### 2.6 Global and pan-African recognition conventions
- **Global Convention on the Recognition of Qualifications concerning Higher Education**: adopted by UNESCO in 2019, in force March 2023. Core principles: a right to a fair, transparent assessment within a reasonable timeframe; recognition unless the awarding body can show a "substantial difference"; the burden of proof on the body denying recognition; a national information centre ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/higher-education/global-convention/about)).
- The **Addis Convention (2014)** is the African regional recognition convention the EAC layer should nest under.

### 2.7 Concrete design patterns for an EAC Teacher Qualification Passport
- **A layered credential:** a CTQF-referenced core portable credential (a "qualified teacher" means the same thing in Juba and Kigali); a stackable ACQF-standard micro-credential layer for CPD and specialisation; a distinct trainer-of-trainers or lead-teacher tier with its own competency set.
- **Recognition process** (from the EU general system): default to recognition; allow a bounded adaptation period or aptitude test only where a substantial difference is documented, never both.
- **"Registered Foreign Teacher" status** (from ASEAN): a fast regional registration that still routes through the host teacher council.
- **A shared digital register plus verification API** across national teacher councils, modelled on the European Professional Card.
- **An alert mechanism** (from the EU directive): a binding obligation to notify all other partner states when a teacher is struck off or restricted, especially for safeguarding offences.
- **Recognition of prior learning** built in from the start, given large numbers of under-qualified or informally trained teachers in South Sudan, Somalia and refugee settings.
- **Anchor, do not reinvent:** build the passport as the EAC implementation of the CTQF, CFSCTP and Addis Convention.

## 3. Regional scorecard precedents

### 3.1 AU CESA indicators
- **CESA 16 to 25** had a dedicated Indicators Manual (2018): a standardised set chosen for fit with the CESA objectives and SDG 4, and for feasibility of collection; data from UIS plus periodic surveys ([CESA Indicators Manual PDF](http://education-au.org/downloads/Guides_and_Toolkits/CESA_Indicators_Manual/CESA_Indicators_Manual_final_July_en.pdf)).
- **CESA 26 to 35**: approved at the AU Summit in February 2025, launched October 2025. Pillars: foundational learning; teacher professionalism; skills for employability; digital learning and innovation; governance and financing. Structure: an investment case, a framework for action (6 strategic areas, 20 objectives), and a monitoring part ([au.int](https://au.int/en/documents/20250616/continental-education-strategy-africa-2026-2035)). An EAC scorecard should map one-to-one onto CESA 26 to 35 so EAC reporting doubles as continental reporting.

### 3.2 SDG 4 and GAML benchmarking mechanics
- The **Global Alliance to Monitor Learning (GAML)**, hosted by UIS, standardises SDG 4 measurement, above all indicator 4.1.1 (share reaching a **Minimum Proficiency Level** in reading and mathematics) ([UIS GAML](https://www.uis.unesco.org/en/gaml)).
- Tools that make data comparable: the **Global Proficiency Framework** and **Policy Linking Toolkit**; standalone **Assessments of Minimum Proficiency Levels**; UIS reporting scales and social moderation to bring PASEC, SEACMEQ and national assessments onto one scale.
- **National SDG 4 benchmarks**: countries set their own 2025 and 2030 target values on seven benchmark indicators; the UIS and GEM SDG 4 Scorecard reports progress with an **on track or off track colour code** against each country's own benchmark. This "self-set target plus traffic-light" mechanic is directly transferable and avoids naming-and-shaming disputes.

### 3.3 Continental Assessment Framework for Africa (CAF-Africa), launched October 2025
- Launched by the Association for Educational Assessment in Africa with the GEM Report, UIS and ADEA. It provides **continental proficiency benchmarks in mathematics and reading at Grades 3, 6 and 9**, a standardised methodology, and capacity development, and is designed to strengthen alignment with, not replace, PASEC and SEACMEQ ([iicba.unesco.org](https://www.iicba.unesco.org/en/launch-continental-assessment-framework-africa-advancing-harmonized-learning-assessment-across)). An EAC learning-outcomes scorecard should adopt the CAF-Africa Grade 3, 6, 9 benchmarks wholesale.

### 3.4 Regional learning-assessment programmes and the comparability lesson
- **SEACMEQ** (Grade 6, Kenya, Tanzania mainland and Zanzibar, Uganda), **PASEC** (Francophone, relevant to Burundi and a bridge for DRC), and **Uwezo and the PAL Network** (citizen-led household assessments, powerful for out-of-school children and advocacy but by design not cross-nationally comparable). Lesson: do not create a new EAC test; **socially moderate** existing data onto the CAF-Africa and UIS scale.

### 3.5 RELI Africa and ALiVE
- **RELI Africa**: a network of 70-plus civil-society organisations across Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda, organised around teacher development, equity and inclusion, values and life skills, and accountability ([reliafrica.org](https://reliafrica.org/)).
- **ALiVE**: household-based assessment of adolescents 13 to 17, in and out of school, measuring self-awareness, problem solving, collaboration and respect, with open-source context-developed tools. A ready-made regional instrument for competencies beyond literacy and numeracy.

### 3.6 Scorecard mechanics that work (distilled)
- A small, locked core indicator set chosen for policy relevance and feasibility of collection.
- Country-set targets plus traffic-light reporting against each country's own benchmark.
- A learning-outcomes pillar anchored to shared benchmarks (CAF-Africa) fed by socially moderated data, not a new regional test.
- A competencies and values module (RELI and ALiVE) and an equity module disaggregated by sex, disability and displacement status.
- An institutional home (an EAC education observatory) that publishes an annual dataset, with independent data alongside country self-report, and a light peer-review step.
- A unique learner identifier and a common EMIS standard as the plumbing.

## 4. Recommended additional gaps

1. **No regional examinations-equating body.** East Africa shared the East African Certificate of Education and an East African Examinations Council until the mid-1970s. Today nothing equates KCSE, Uganda's UCE and UACE, Tanzania's CSEE and ACSEE, Rwanda's advanced level, and Burundi's baccalauréat. A mobile 16-year-old cannot transfer mid-secondary.
2. **Early childhood and school-starting-age harmonisation.** Foundational learning depends on pre-primary, yet states differ on entry age, duration and whether pre-primary is compulsory or free. A mobile 5-year-old can lose or repeat a year at a border.
3. **Teacher labour-market planning and social-security portability.** Kenya and Tanzania train more teachers than they hire; South Sudan, Somalia and eastern DRC face acute shortages. There is no regional deployment or exchange facility and no totalisation agreement so pension and social-security contributions follow a teacher who moves.
4. **Portability of student finance and "national student" tuition treatment.** National student-loan schemes are not portable, and universities charge non-nationals higher fees even within the Common Market. There is no EAC Erasmus equivalent.
5. **Quality assurance of cross-border and transnational provision.** Branch campuses and franchised programmes operate with no shared register of recognised institutions and no mechanism to circulate a blocklist of degree mills. Kenya's qualifications authority has flagged over 10,000 fake certificates.
6. **Digital learning interoperability and a learner-record architecture.** No EAC standard for learning management systems, no common open-educational-resources licensing policy, no interoperable digital-credential wallet, no shared edtech data standard, no regional OER repository, and no unique learner identifier.
7. **A safeguarding and professional-conduct alert mechanism** (distinct from registry interoperability): a binding rule that a teacher struck off for misconduct in one state cannot cross a border and re-register.
8. **Recognition of non-formal, religious and accelerated education** within the qualifications framework (Quranic and madrasa education, church-run complementary basic education, accelerated programmes for over-age learners).
9. **Nomadic, pastoralist and border-community education.** Mutual-enrolment rules, a shared mobile-learner curriculum, and portable records for children who are regional rather than national.
10. **Extreme heterogeneity and "variable geometry" of an 8-member EAC.** Every existing instrument was built for 5 to 6 Anglophone states. Harmonisation on a single timeline is not feasible. Build in differentiated timelines and a coalition-of-the-willing pilot mechanism, with later accession clauses.
11. **Sustainability and financing of the harmonisation machinery itself.** IUCEA, EASTECO and EAKC are heavily donor-dependent and face contribution arrears. Line-fund the integration institutions from assessed contributions.
12. **Research and doctoral training harmonisation** (joint and double degrees, common supervision standards, mutual recognition of research-ethics clearance, cross-border research-data governance).
13. **The regional learning-materials market.** Textbook publishers face up to eight separate approval and procurement regimes. A common approval track and a regional procurement window would lower costs.
14. **Mutual recognition of basic-school accreditation and inspection**, which a mobile family needs when choosing a school across a border.
15. **A Kiswahili and instructional-language proficiency scale.** A CEFR-style regional scale and a teacher language-certification standard, so "can teach in Kiswahili" is verifiable and transferable. The same for English and French.
16. **Cross-border data-sharing rules for education records.** A regional adequacy or standard-contractual-clause regime for education data, given divergent national laws and localisation requirements.
17. **Alignment with IGAD on refugee education.** Kenya, Uganda, South Sudan and Somalia are also bound by the IGAD Djibouti Declaration on Refugee Education (2017). An EAC system that does not interoperate with it will duplicate or contradict it.
18. **Domestic financing floor and cost-sharing formula.** African education spending averages about 4.1 percent of GDP against the 4 to 6 percent (up to 7) Incheon benchmark. Adopt a regional financing floor as a scorecard indicator, plus a transparent cost-sharing formula for shared institutions.

## 5. Design implications for the EAC Education System

**A. Build a translation device, not an imposed system.** Adopt an 8-to-10-level regional meta-framework that references the ACQF and CTQF, and require each partner state (DRC and Somalia on longer timelines) to produce a referencing report.

**B. Operationalise what already exists before creating anything new.** Adopt EACATS and issue the East African Graduation Statement; approve the 8-state TVET Qualifications Framework; convert the 2014 harmonised curriculum drafts into a lightweight common structure (cycle lengths, entry ages, exit competencies); extend Article 11 MRAs to teachers.

**C. Teacher Qualification Passport = layered credential plus shared register plus alert mechanism.** Pair it with pension and social-security totalisation and a teacher-deployment facility that routes surplus teachers to deficit and crisis areas.

**D. One regional scorecard, small and CESA-aligned.** A locked core set mapped to CESA 26 to 35 and the seven SDG 4 benchmark indicators; country-set targets with traffic-light reporting; a learning-outcomes pillar anchored to CAF-Africa Grade 3, 6, 9 and fed by socially moderated data; a competencies and values module; equity disaggregation; published annually by an observatory. Precede it with a common EMIS standard and a unique learner identifier.

**E. Fix the plumbing: data, verification, financing.** Negotiate a regional education-data-sharing instrument; build a shared positive register of recognised institutions and a degree-mill blocklist; line-fund the integration institutions from assessed contributions with an equalisation window for the poorest members.

**F. Give the framework teeth, and sequence it politically.** Embed the system in a Protocol or Common Market Annex with a binding timetable, justiciable before the East African Court of Justice, plus a recognition ombudsperson. Use variable geometry: start with a coalition of the willing, with accession clauses for the others. Protect against brain drain with deployment incentives and benefit portability.

**G. Nest inside continental and global instruments.** Design every component as the EAC's regional implementation of the AU CTQF and CFSCTP, the ACQF, the Addis Convention, the UNESCO Global Convention, CESA 26 to 35 and CAF-Africa.
