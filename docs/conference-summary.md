# The 2nd EAC Regional Education Conference

**Nairobi, Kenya, 24 to 28 August 2026** (pre-conference workshops from 25 August; main conference 26 to 28 August). Venue: Kenyatta International Convention Centre, Jambo Hall. More than 600 delegates from the eight EAC partner states.

**Full theme:** *Transforming Education in East Africa: From Commitments to Impact, Fostering Resilience and Future-Ready Systems for Sustainable Development.*

Convened by the EAC Secretariat with RELI Africa, ADEA, IUCEA, GIZ, UNICEF, UNESCO and partners.

## The eight EAC partner states

Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania (including Zanzibar), Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, Somalia (acceded 2024), Democratic Republic of Congo (acceded 2022).

## The first conference

Arusha, Tanzania, 12 to 15 August 2024. Theme: *Educate an African Fit for the 21st Century: Collective Action for Quality, Inclusive, and Life-long Learning in East Africa.* A central resolution was to establish an **EAC Regional Forum on Education** as a standing multi-stakeholder body. The 2026 conference was framed as the two-year review, "From Resolutions to Results."

## The nine sub-themes

1. **Foundational Learning**
2. **Teacher Capacity and Professional Development**
3. **Inclusive Education**
4. **Leveraging EdTech and Digitalisation**
5. **TVET and Youth Agency**
6. **Financing, Accountability and Governance**
7. **Data-Driven Decision-Making and Evidence-Based Policies**
8. **Regional and Continental Partnerships**
9. **Higher Education** (Advancing the East African Common Higher Education Area)

## Featured programme components

- **Opening Ceremony** with the Chief Guest from the Office of the President of Kenya; welcome by the EAC Secretary General, the Deputy Secretary General, the Chair of the Sectoral Council on Education, Science and Technology, Culture and Sports (Uganda), and the Chair of the EAC Council of Ministers.
- **Ministerial Roundtable:** *From Resolutions to Results: Two Years of Progress Across the EAC Education Agenda*, with education ministers and senior officials from all partner states, chaired by Dr Sara Ruto.
- **Permanent Secretary Plenary Panel:** *Charting the Way Forward: Coordinated Action for Advancing a Regional Education Agenda.*
- **Keynote:** policy insights from the 2026 Global Education Monitoring (GEM) Report.
- **Sub-theme spotlight sessions**, including: *Advancing the East African Common Higher Education Area* (IUCEA); *Delivering on the Promise of Foundational Learning: What Does it Take?* (ADEA); *Promoting Cross-Border Data Sharing and Joint Research Initiatives Within East Africa*; *From Regional Cooperation to Peer Learning: Strengthening Education Reform Across the EAC*.
- **Pre-conference workshops**, including: *From Data to Decisions: Transforming the Education Governance Landscape in East Africa* (ADEA); *Beyond the Numbers: Making Sense of Learning Assessment Data* (UNICEF, FLAT+ and LSDP Lab); *Scaling the ICAN and ICAR Initiative to Close the Foundational Data Gap in East Africa* (PAL Network, Uwezo, Usawa Agenda); *Transforming Teacher Professional Development through Digital Communities of Practice and Competency-Based Mentorship* (University of Rwanda); *Artificial Intelligence in Education: From Awareness to Institutional Optimization* (UNESCO, IUCEA, GIZ).
- **Parallel paper sessions** across all nine sub-themes, and **roundtables and fireside chats** including *From localisation to Scale: Strengthening Socio-Emotional Learning in Early and Foundational Education* and *From Data Foundations to AI-Powered Education: Scaling Digital Public Infrastructure in Rwanda*.
- **Closing Ceremony:** reading of the Conference Communiqué and Partner States Resolutions.

## Recurring threads across the programme

- Harmonisation of curricula, qualifications, teacher development and assessment across the bloc, so a qualified teacher and a learner's record can move across borders.
- Foundational literacy and numeracy, social and emotional learning, and turning data into decisions.
- The gap between commitments made at conferences and summits and implementation on the ground, especially at the district or "middle tier."
- Inclusion of learners with disabilities, girls, refugees and displaced children, and pastoralist and marginalised communities.
- AI arriving in classrooms ahead of infrastructure, teacher capacity, and shared rules on children's data.
