# The gap register

What a unified EAC education system has to close. Each gap is tagged by **source**:

- **[Floor]** raised on the conference floor
- **[Brief]** named in the commissioning brief
- **[Review]** surfaced by the three-track advisory review (see the other files in this folder)

---

## Learning

| Gap | Source | System response |
|---|---|---|
| No shared definition of foundational proficiency; citizen-led tools and national assessments are not equated | Floor | A common minimum-proficiency standard for reading and numeracy, aligned to the Global Proficiency Framework and the Continental Assessment Framework for Africa (Grades 3, 6, 9). |
| Foundational learning is not integrated across the region (SEL, data and evidence, ICT sit apart, no single framework or roadmap) | Brief | The EAC Foundational Learning Framework: three pillars, a playful pedagogy spine, a competency crosswalk, a pre-primary to upper-primary progression. |
| Early childhood is treated as optional; pre-primary access, financing and workforce are the weakest link | Review | Pre-primary is the first rung of the progression, with a minimum service definition, a trained workforce plan, and a school-readiness check (IDELA or MELQO). |
| Language-of-instruction policy diverges (Kiswahili, English, French, Arabic; different transition years); decodable local-language readers are scarce | Review | A regional language-of-instruction protocol, assessment in the language of instruction, and a pooled local-language reader library in the commons. |
| Over-age and out-of-school children are missed by a curriculum-paced, school-based system | Review | A regional catch-up and accelerated-learning standard, and household-based assessment so out-of-school children still count. |

## Teachers

| Gap | Source | System response |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher development is episodic and uneven | Floor | A regional teacher competency standard, a tiered professional pathway, and digital communities of practice built into the passport. |
| No recognised "East African teacher" | Brief | The Teacher Qualification Passport: a universal competency assessment leading to a portable, tiered credential. |
| No teacher mutual recognition agreement (the EAC has MRAs for accountants, engineers, architects, vets, none for teachers) | Review | A Teachers MRA under Common Market Protocol Article 11, drafted against the AU Continental Teacher Qualification Framework, with the passport as its instrument. |
| Teacher registries cannot verify each other; certificate fraud is hard to catch; no shared misconduct list | Review | A federated teacher registry index, a credential-verification service, and a binding cross-notification alert when a teacher is struck off. |
| Mobility could drain the poorest districts; pensions and social security do not follow a teacher across borders | Review | Hard-to-staff service as a passport tier requirement, a totalisation arrangement for benefits, and a regional deployment facility. |

## Data and AI

| Gap | Source | System response |
|---|---|---|
| No single regional data warehouse; data lives in country silos | Brief | A federated warehouse: states keep custody, a shared semantic layer makes data comparable, three access tiers govern who sees what. |
| Data does not become decisions | Brief | An indicator to insight to brief to commitment to impact workflow, with an AI assistant that answers in plain language and cites the source indicator. |
| AI is arriving in classrooms with no safety floor | Brief | A regional AI-in-classrooms safeguarding standard (UNESCO guidance, AU Continental AI Strategy) plus a procurement checklist. |
| Child-data protection is not harmonised (South Sudan has no law; others differ) | Review | A child-data minimisation standard, aggregation thresholds, a pseudonymous per-state learner key, and alignment to the EAC cross-border data flows mechanism. |
| The middle tier has data it cannot use | Review | A middle-tier dashboard as a first-class product of the warehouse, plus a data-use capability track. |

## Mobility and equivalency

| Gap | Source | System response |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-border teaching is not practical (recognition, registration, standards) | Brief | Passport plus Teachers MRA plus a regional supply-and-exchange platform for placements and secondments. |
| No basic-education equivalency table for mobile and displaced learners; the region lost its shared certificate and examinations council in the mid-1970s | Review | A basic-education equivalency matrix by grade and phase, a lightweight examinations-equating function, and a portable learner record. |
| School years and examination calendars do not line up | Review | A published regional assessment calendar and a phased move toward compatible term structures. |

## Equity and inclusion

| Gap | Source | System response |
|---|---|---|
| Children with disabilities are unseen and unassessed | Floor | A regional inclusive-assessment toolkit and accommodations standard (Washington Group modules), and disability-disaggregated scorecard indicators. |
| Crisis and refugee education is treated as an exception; refugee-teacher certification and cross-border continuity sit outside the system | Review | Crisis contexts as a design case throughout: portable learner records, refugee-teacher certification routes, low-tech delivery, and interoperability with the IGAD Djibouti Declaration on Refugee Education. |
| Gender parity is measured, gender outcomes are not | Review | Outcome and safety indicators disaggregated by sex, with a comply-or-explain line in the scorecard. |
| Parents, learners and communities are not really engaged (Ubuntu stated but not operationalised) | Brief | A stakeholder engagement model with a defined information path to parents and community organisations, and learner and parent voice in the scorecard. |

## System and financing

| Gap | Source | System response |
|---|---|---|
| Information does not flow after the conference | Brief | A published dissemination architecture and a live communiqué tracker: every resolution has an owner, a milestone and a status. |
| Learning resources and research are not shared | Brief | An open resource and research commons with a regional licence, curation, and a translation pool. |
| No funding model for the regional system itself | Review | A blended model: partner-state contributions on a GDP-weighted formula, a pooled donor window, cost-recovery on premium services. |
| Non-state, private and faith-based schools are outside regional quality assurance | Review | Registration and minimum-standard hooks that apply to any provider drawing on the commons or issuing recognised results. |
| Commitments have no teeth (the Community works by consensus) | Review | A peer-review and comply-or-explain instrument owned by the Regional Forum on Education, reporting to the Sectoral Council. |
| One timeline for eight very different states will fail (Francophone DRC, Arabic and Somali Somalia, fragile South Sudan) | Review | Variable geometry: a coalition of the willing starts, with differentiated timelines, targeted support, and open accession clauses. |
| Climate shocks close schools with no regional response | Review | A shared education continuity protocol and a regional disaster-risk financing facility line in the roadmap. |

---

*The commissioning brief also asked for the components already reflected above: regional teacher upskilling and tooling, a universal scorecard and key indicators, a regional large-scale competency assessment, cross-border skill share, a data portal with an assistant, foundational learning integration, a community of practice, learning-resource and research sharing, regional exchanges beyond conferences, policy and compliance harmonisation, a qualification passport with a trainer-of-trainers tier, AI safety and governance, a beyond-conference stakeholder strategy, a common knowledge base, a clear roadmap for foundational learning, an "East African teacher" brand, better information dissemination, and turning data into decisions.*
