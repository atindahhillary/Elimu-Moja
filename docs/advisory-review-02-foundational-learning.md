# Advisory review 2: foundational learning, assessment, SEL, and playful and low-tech pedagogy

> AI-assisted desk research prepared to inform the Elimu Moja Foundational Learning Framework. Verify every claim against the primary source before use. Links are inline.

A note on "FLARA": the regional foundational-learning assessment agenda is real and is carried by several named initiatives (PAL Network ICAN and ICAR; ADEA's AFLAI; the AU Continental Assessment Framework for Africa; RELI Africa's ALiVE and ADAPT). The exact membership and status of a body under the name "FLARA" should be confirmed directly with PAL Network and RELI Africa or Zizi Afrique.

## 1. Regional foundational-learning assessment landscape in East Africa

### 1.1 Citizen-led and household-based assessments (the East African tradition)
- **Uwezo** (Kiswahili for "capability"): launched 2009 as a programme of Twaweza East Africa, modelled on Pratham and ASER (India). Household-based, oral, one-on-one assessment of all children aged 6 to 16 against a Grade 2 competence level in literacy and basic numeracy; volunteers give instant feedback to households. Run annually across Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania ([palnetwork.org](https://palnetwork.org/uwezo-uganda/), [acer.org](https://www.acer.org/files/AssessGEMs_Uwezo.pdf)). Now delivered by Usawa Agenda (Kenya), Uwezo Uganda and Uwezo Tanzania.
- **Usawa Agenda** (Kenya), successor to Uwezo Kenya: 2024 and 2025 findings show 41 percent of Grade 7 learners lack basic competencies in both literacy and numeracy; 29 percent of Grade 9 students lack foundational competencies ([usawaagenda.org](https://usawaagenda.org/learning-assessment-reports/)).
- **PAL Network** (17 members, 15 countries; secretariat in Nairobi) built shared instruments so citizen-led data can be compared:
  - **ICAN** (International Common Assessment of Numeracy) and **ICAR or ICARe** (reading: listening comprehension, decoding, reading comprehension). Oral, one-on-one, household-based; capture in-school and out-of-school children; positioned as a candidate common metric for SDG 4.1.1(a) ([palnetwork.org](https://palnetwork.org/ican-icar/)).
  - 2025 cycle: more than 89,000 children in over 59,000 households across 12 countries including Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. A second cycle is planned for 2027 to 2028.
  - **PAL-ELANA** (Early Language, Literacy and Numeracy Assessment): an early-years instrument, final data collection completed 2024.
- **Zizi Afrique Foundation** (Kenya) is the PAL Network member for Kenya and a regional hub for assessment innovation.

### 1.2 Life-skills, values and SEL assessments born in East Africa
- **ALiVE** (Assessment of Life Skills and Values in East Africa): an initiative of the Values and Life Skills group of RELI Africa, coordinated by Zizi Afrique, involving 70-plus organisations. First large-scale household-based assessment of life skills and values covering in-school and out-of-school adolescents aged 13 to 17 in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. Measures **problem solving, self-awareness, collaboration, and respect**, contextualised through rapid ethnographic interviews ("the ALiVE Way"); tools are open-source. 2022 round assessed 45,442 adolescents; results are low (for example only about 5 percent of assessed Kenyan adolescents were proficient in problem solving) ([reliafrica.org/alive](https://reliafrica.org/alive/), [globalpartnership.org](https://www.globalpartnership.org/blog/new-assessment-foundational-life-skills-east-africa)). Sister project **ADAPT** works on getting 21st-century-skills assessment into national policy.
- **CoMSELA** (a Common or Contextualised Measure of Social and Emotional Learning in Africa): extends the ALiVE approach to primary-school-age children (piloted with Grades 4 to 6 in Kenya), combining self-report with performance and scenario-based tasks ([teachforkenya.org](https://teachforkenya.org/unlocking-leadership-through-social-emotional-learning-insights-from-the-comsela-pilot-study/)). Related ground-up African SEL measurement includes Jukes et al. in Tanzania.

### 1.3 System-level and cross-national assessments
- **SEACMEQ**: Grade 6 reading and mathematics, roughly 5 to 6 year cycles, members include Kenya, Tanzania (mainland and Zanzibar) and Uganda.
- **PASEC** (CONFEMEN): Grade 2 and end-of-primary reading and mathematics in Francophone Africa; relevant to Burundi and, prospectively, DRC and South Sudan.
- **EGRA and EGMA** (USAID and RTI, open-source): oral early-grade reading and mathematics batteries; the backbone of Tusome and PRIMR in Kenya and Jifunze work in Tanzania.
- **MICS Foundational Learning Module** (UNICEF): household literacy and numeracy for ages 7 to 14 every roughly 3 years.
- **National assessments**: KNEC (Kenya, including KPSEA and KJSEA), NECTA (Tanzania), UNEB (Uganda), REB and NESA (Rwanda).

### 1.4 Structured pedagogy and remediation programmes
- **Tusome** ("Let's Read"), Kenya (USAID and RTI, national): structured lesson guides, tablet-based instructional coaching, benchmarked EGRA. Working in 23,000-plus schools, roughly 7.8 million Grade 1 to 3 children; roughly tripled the share of Grade 2 pupils meeting the oral reading fluency benchmark ([rti.org](https://www.rti.org/announcements/tusome-%E2%80%9Clet%E2%80%99s-read%E2%80%9D-program-achieves-impressive-gains-child-literacy-across-kenya)).
- **Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) Africa**: Pratham and J-PAL joint venture, Nairobi, since 2016; groups children by demonstrated level rather than grade for a dedicated remedial block. Global Education Evidence Advisory Panel rates it among the most cost-effective interventions; adapted in 17-plus African countries reaching roughly 7 million children.
- **Structured pedagogy debate**: strong causal evidence for scaled structured pedagogy in sub-Saharan Africa, but a live critique that full scripting reduces teacher autonomy. Recent syntheses favour "**structured but not fully scripted**" designs plus instructional coaching ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11422479/)).

### 1.5 Continental architecture to dock into
- **ADEA foundational-learning work**: African basic-education ministers prioritised foundational learning for the AU Year of Education (2024); AU commitment to end learning poverty by 2035; **FLIGHT** (Foundational Learning Initiative for Government-Led Transformation); the annual **Foundational Learning Exchange (FLEX)**.
- **AFLAI** (Africa Foundational Learning Assessment Initiative): Africa-led, ADEA with Human Capital Africa; provides technical assistance to governments across six pathways (policy dialogue, assessment architecture, instrument development, implementation, classroom data use, global benchmarking) ([aflai.org](https://aflai.org/)).
- **Continental Assessment Framework for Africa (CAF-Africa)**: launched October 2025; sets continental proficiency benchmarks in mathematics and reading at Grades 3, 6 and 9 and coordinates PASEC and SEACMEQ.
- **Global Proficiency Framework and Minimum Proficiency Levels**: MPLa (about Grade 2 or 3), MPLb (about Grade 5), MPLc (about Grade 8) map to SDG 4.1.1 reporting.
- **EAC base**: partner states have flagged harmonisation of curricula, standards and assessment as a priority; KICD holds draft Harmonised Curriculum Structures for primary and secondary education.

### 1.6 Comparability problems for a regional competency assessment
Different reference grades (2 vs 3 vs 6); different languages of testing; school-based instruments miss the out-of-school population (roughly one in three 12 to 14 year-olds and three in five 15 to 17 year-olds are out of school in sub-Saharan Africa); donor-funded cycles that stop when a project ends; and life-skills and SEL constructs barely covered by any system assessment. A regional assessment can be built by (a) adopting the GPF and MPL and CAF-Africa Grade 3, 6, 9 benchmarks as the common yardstick, (b) using PAL ICAN and ICARe and PAL-ELANA as the household-based, out-of-school-inclusive layer, (c) using ALiVE and CoMSELA-style contextualised performance tasks for SEL, (d) linking national assessments to the regional metric through equating studies, and (e) publishing results in a standing regional barometer.

## 2. Curriculum competency frameworks

### 2.1 Kenya: the seven CBC or CBE core competencies
1. **Communication and collaboration**
2. **Critical thinking and problem solving**
3. **Imagination and creativity**
4. **Citizenship**
5. **Digital literacy**
6. **Learning to learn**
7. **Self-efficacy**

Core values: love, responsibility, respect, unity, peace, patriotism, social justice, integrity, anchored in eight National Goals of Education. **Pertinent and Contemporary Issues** are mainstreamed across all learning areas in four clusters, including a **life skills and values** cluster covering social-emotional competencies.

### 2.2 Kenya: the shift from CBC to CBE (post-2024)
The **Presidential Working Party on Education Reform** (2022 to 2023) led to the Basic Education (CBE) Sessional Paper of 2024. Key changes: a rebrand from "Curriculum" to "Education" (infrastructure, teacher development, assessment and financing must all be competency-based); the **2-6-3-3-3** structure (2 years pre-primary, 6 primary, 3 Junior School domiciled in primary schools, 3 Senior School, 3 tertiary); rationalisation of learning areas; school-based assessment plus national assessments (KPSEA at Grade 6, KJSEA at Grade 9); the first Grade 9 cohort transitions to Senior School in January 2026 into three pathways (STEM; Social Sciences; Arts and Sports Science).

### 2.3 Uganda: Lower Secondary Competence-Based Curriculum (from 2020)
Framework elements: Key Learning Outcomes, Values, Generic Skills, Cross-cutting Issues, and the subject menu. **Generic skills**: critical thinking and problem solving; creativity and innovation; communication; co-operation and self-directed learning; and mathematical computation and ICT proficiency. Primary P1 to P3 still runs a **thematic curriculum taught in area local languages**, with an Accelerated Education Programme for over-age learners.

### 2.4 Tanzania: competence-based curriculum (2023 policy, 2024 rollout)
The 2023 Education and Training Policy triggered a new curriculum rolled out from January 2024 for pre-primary, Standard 1 and Standard 3, with a vocational stream option at Form 1. Persistent constraints: teacher preparation, class sizes, materials, and a language-of-instruction divide (Kiswahili in primary, English in secondary).

### 2.5 Rwanda: Competence-Based Curriculum (2015, rollout 2016)
**Generic competences**: critical thinking; creativity and innovation; research and problem solving; communication; co-operation, interpersonal management and life skills; and lifelong learning. Cross-cutting issues include peace and values education, genocide studies, environment, gender, inclusive education. English is the medium of instruction from Primary 1.

### 2.6 Implication for a pan-regional framework
A strong shared spine across all four systems: **communication, collaboration, critical thinking and problem solving, creativity and innovation, citizenship and values, digital or ICT skills, and learning-to-learn or lifelong learning.** Divergences to accommodate: Kenya's standalone "self-efficacy" and "digital literacy"; Uganda's bundled "mathematical computation and ICT proficiency"; Rwanda's separation of "research and problem solving" from "critical thinking"; and four language-of-instruction regimes. Express the framework as a **competency-and-progression map keyed to the common spine**, with a country annex.

## 3. SEL frameworks and measurement for African primary contexts
- **CASEL 5**: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making, with "transformative SEL" foregrounding identity, agency and equity. US-origin; needs contextualisation for East Africa.
- **Harvard EASEL Lab "Explore SEL"**: a crosswalk mapping 40-plus SEL frameworks onto a common taxonomy, useful for reconciling CASEL with Kenya's "self-efficacy", Rwanda's "life skills" and ALiVE's four constructs.
- **"Measuring What Matters" and breadth of skills** (Brookings): the Learning Metrics Task Force domains; the Breadth of Learning Opportunities tools; work on assessing 21st-century skills.
- **INEE PSS-SEL**: guidance and a measurement inventory for psychosocial support and SEL in crisis contexts, relevant for refugee-hosting areas.
- **Think Equal**: a structured, narrative-based SEL curriculum for ages 3 to 6, with RCT evidence and a model of scaling through government systems.
- **Ubongo Learning** (Tanzania): "Akili and Me" teaches socio-emotional skills alongside pre-literacy and numeracy; a co-production with the IRC created caregiver-facing SEL content for refugee settings.

**How SEL is assessed at scale, and the limits.** Self-report is cheapest but suffers social-desirability and cross-cultural reference-group bias. Teacher report is feasible but carries halo effects. Direct performance and scenario-based assessment (the ALiVE and CoMSELA approach) is more valid but costlier and needs strong enumerator training. **Practical stance**: a mixed-method SEL module (short contextualised self-report plus one or two performance tasks plus a brief teacher rating), reported as a descriptive "life-skills and wellbeing" band rather than a single score.

## 4. Playful and low-tech pedagogies
- **Learning through Play** (LEGO Foundation): five characteristics: the experience is **joyful, meaningful, actively engaging, iterative, and socially interactive**. The "Learning through play at school" white paper identifies eight playful integrated pedagogies (active, collaborative, cooperative, experiential, guided discovery, inquiry-based, problem-based, project-based learning).
- **Six Bricks** (Care for Education, South Africa, with the LEGO Foundation): six DUPLO-type bricks in six colours used for short daily teacher-led activities; builds executive function, working memory, self-regulation, fine-motor control, and language and numeracy readiness. Low unit cost, no electricity or connectivity requirement. Introduced into East African classrooms through PlayMatters since 2023.
- **PlayMatters** (IRC and LEGO Foundation): a roughly 100 million US dollar programme delivering learning through play to pre-primary and lower-primary children affected by crisis in Ethiopia, Uganda and Tanzania, in refugee and host communities; target roughly 800,000 children and 10,000 teachers.
- **CS Unplugged** (University of Canterbury): teaches computer-science concepts and computational thinking through games, cards and movement with no computer required; explicitly designed to cross the divide between information-rich and information-poor contexts.
- **Physical or 3D Scratch blocks**: Ghana has deployed 3D-printed Scratch blocks so rural pupils without computers can learn coding logic hands-on. **Scratch block coding** builds computational thinking; blended teacher CPD for Scratch has been piloted in Western Kenya.

**Evidence pattern**: play-based and unplugged approaches show consistent gains in executive function, engagement, social-emotional skills and problem solving, with academic effects strongest when the playful pedagogy is tightly linked to specific literacy or numeracy goals and teachers get sustained coaching.

## 5. ICT integration for FLN in low-infrastructure settings
- **Nyansapo AI** (Kenya): a mobile app that listens to a child read, flags mispronounced words, and produces reading-ability profiles for teachers; the assessment is modelled on the Uwezo instrument; used to support level-based grouping despite teacher shortages.
- **EIDU** (Kenya, with pilots elsewhere): an open-source, offline-capable Android app on sub-50-dollar phones, combining adaptive learner content with structured-pedagogy teacher lesson guides and anonymised government dashboards; RCT evidence of literacy and numeracy gains in pre-primary.
- **onebillion, onecourse, onetab**: a solar-chargeable tablet plus child-directed literacy and numeracy software in the child's own language and at the child's own level; nine-plus RCTs (Malawi, Zambia, Sierra Leone, Rwanda, Bangladesh) show significant gains with girls progressing equally to boys; scaled by Imagine Worldwide, including Malawi's national BEFIT programme with GPE.
- **QR-coded or audio-linked textbooks** (Somalia): printed books carrying QR codes linking to audio, video or self-marking exercises, as a low-tech bridge where connectivity and devices are scarce. Relevant to South Sudan, northern Kenya, Karamoja and Somali-region border areas.
- **AI teacher diagnostics**: teacher-facing tools that identify each learner's foundational gap and build longitudinal profiles. The tool referred to as **TABASAM** sits in this category; its specifics could not be verified in public sources and should be confirmed with the implementer.
- **Digital-readiness assessment frameworks**: the World Bank **EdTech Readiness Index (ETRI)** measures school-level practices and system policies across six components. A regional framework should require ETRI-style readiness data before scaling any device-dependent FLN intervention.

**Evidence pattern for ICT and FLN**: the strongest results come from (a) child-directed adaptive software for a fixed daily dose on rugged, solar-charged hardware, (b) AI assessment that saves teacher time on one-on-one FLN checks, and (c) tools that feed simple, actionable data to teachers and officials. Results collapse without electricity and device-management plans, local-language content, and teacher support.

## 6. Recommended additional gaps for the framework

1. A shared FLN learning-progression and common minimum-proficiency definitions, keyed to the Global Proficiency Framework and CAF-Africa Grade 3, 6, 9 benchmarks.
2. An explicit language-of-instruction and mother-tongue transition policy, with decodable local-language readers and teacher language-proficiency standards.
3. Pre-primary and ECD access and quality, including a play-based quality standard and a school-readiness plus executive-function assessment module (IDELA or MELQO; Kenya's Tayari model as a reference).
4. A regional position on structured pedagogy: structured but not fully scripted, plus instructional coaching.
5. Catch-up and accelerated learning for over-age and out-of-school children (INEE Accelerated Education Working Group standards).
6. Inclusive assessment for children with disabilities (Washington Group and UNICEF Child Functioning and Inclusive Education Modules; accommodations; disaggregated reporting).
7. Data comparability, equating studies, and a public annual EAC Learning Barometer.
8. Teacher content knowledge diagnosis and targeted CPD in early reading and early mathematics.
9. Structured parental and community engagement in FLN (household feedback, home reading, caregiver SEL, community score-cards).
10. Population-scale measurement of executive function and school readiness (IDELA or MELQO).
11. Equal priority for early numeracy alongside early reading.
12. Teacher and system assessment literacy and formative use of data.
13. Sustainable domestic financing and a permanent regional assessment function.
14. Teacher wellbeing and teachers' own SEL competencies.
15. Local-language book supply (ending the "book famine").
16. Unique learner identifiers for multi-year, cross-border learning trajectories.
17. Climate-resilience and displacement continuity planning for FLN.
18. AI and child-data governance standards for edtech used with young learners.

## 7. Design implications for the EAC Foundational Learning Framework
1. Organise around a common competency spine, with a country annex mapping Kenya's seven competencies, Uganda's five generic skills, Rwanda's generic competences and Tanzania's set.
2. Anchor the three pillars the brief specifies and define each measurably: **SEL** (contextualised ALiVE and CoMSELA constructs, cross-walked to CASEL 5, mixed-method module); **Data and evidence** (GPF and MPL and CAF-Africa benchmarks; a household-based, out-of-school-inclusive layer; equating studies; a public annual barometer); **ICT integration** (ETRI-style readiness first; child-directed adaptive software on rugged solar hardware with local-language content; AI oral-reading assessment; low-tech bridges; child-data and equity standards).
3. Make pedagogy explicit: Learning through Play and the eight playful pedagogies as default; Six Bricks as a standard low-cost executive-function routine; CS Unplugged and physical or 3D Scratch blocks for digital literacy where there are no devices; structured-but-not-scripted lesson support with coaching.
4. Start before Grade 1 (a pre-primary and ECD component with a school-readiness and executive-function module) and extend past the on-track child (an accelerated or catch-up component).
5. Take a position on language of early literacy, of instruction and of assessment, with a decodable-readers supply plan.
6. Design for inclusion and equity from the start (Washington Group modules, accommodations, disability-disaggregated reporting; explicit planning for refugee-hosting, ASAL, pastoralist and climate-displaced learners).
7. Dock into continental architecture (AU LEARN and CAF-Africa, ADEA's AFLAI and FLIGHT, the AU 2035 learning-poverty goal), do not duplicate it.
8. Fund it domestically and permanently (national FLN budget lines and a standing regional assessment function).
