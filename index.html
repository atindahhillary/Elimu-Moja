The opening

The region agreed. It just hasn’t reached the classroom.

The 2nd EAC Regional Education Conference put foundational learning, social and emotional learning, and parental engagement at the centre of the agenda, and named the same method every time: play. That consensus is real. Right now it lives in communiqués.

1

What was said

Prioritise foundational learning and SEL, involve parents, and get there through playful, child-centred teaching.

2

What is missing

A concrete programme teachers can run, comparable across countries, with the training and measurement already attached.

3

What SELution adds

Four play-based strands on one pathway, designed for low-resource classrooms, ready to pilot with partners already asking.

The challenge, in numbers

The region is not behind on coding and robotics. It has not started.

Across the eight EAC partner states, an illustrative 0.7% of schools have a robotics kit in pupils’ hands and 1.4% run any structured coding activity, plugged or unplugged. Underneath that, roughly one Grade 3 pupil in four reads at grade level and about one primary school in seven timetables and assesses social and emotional learning. These are not four separate problems: a child who cannot read a sequence of instructions cannot debug one, and a classroom without emotional safety cannot sustain the collaborative, failure-heavy work that coding and robotics demand. SELution is built for that whole stack, and it starts where the numbers are lowest.

    The offer

    Four strands, one pathway

    Each strand is a set of structured play activities with a clear learning target, a teacher script, and simple materials. They share one training pathway and one progress record, so a school runs them as a single programme.

    Strand 01

    Social & emotional learning

    Naming feelings, turn-taking, calming routines, friendship and conflict repair. The backbone the other three strands lean on.

    A session looks like A 20-minute circle game: children pass a feelings ball or a Six Bricks tower, act out a scenario, and agree one class rule. No printing needed.
    Strand 02

    Foundational literacy & numeracy

    Letter sounds, decoding, number sense and early operations through games, songs and manipulatives, mapped to the national curriculum.

    A session looks like Teams build words or number bonds from Six Bricks and bottle-cap tiles against a timer, then teach their answer to another team. The score is the assessment.
    Strand 03

    Unplugged coding

    Sequencing, loops, debugging and algorithms with no device: floor grids, arrow cards, and children as the robot following each other’s instructions.

    A session looks like One child writes an arrow-card program, another walks the grid, the class debugs the wrong turns together. Pure logic, zero electricity.
    Strand 04

    Robotics

    The same computational thinking, now with a physical build: low-cost motors, recycled materials and a small kit shared across a cluster of schools.

    A session looks like Groups build and program a line-follower or a grabber from a shared kit, present it, and pass the kit to the next school on a rota.

    Strands 03 and 04 are one continuum: unplugged coding needs nothing, robotics adds a shared kit when a cluster is ready. A school can start with strands 01 and 02 alone.

    The method

    Play is the spine, not a decoration

    Construction, not instruction. Children build understanding by making and re-making, not by being told. Every strand runs on the same five-beat cycle, which is what makes four strands feel like one programme and what the evidence says actually shifts foundational outcomes.

    Goalone target
    Gamea rule to play by
    Rolesevery child in
    Reflectwhat did we learn
    Record10-second note

    The record at the end feeds one simple progress view per child, aggregated to class, school and cluster. That is the measurement spine partners and funders see.

    Six Bricks

    Six DUPLO bricks on every desk. Short games that wake up the brain and build self-regulation, curiosity, memory, sequencing and early numeracy. The workhorse of the SEL and literacy strands.

    Play method

    Floor grids & arrow cards

    Sequencing, loops and debugging drawn in chalk, with children as the robot. No device, no power.

    Unplugged coding

    Bottle-cap tiles

    A free, endless manipulative for letters, sounds, number bonds and place value. Every school already has the caps.

    Literacy & numeracy

    Recycled-parts builds

    Motors, wheels and card from a shared kit. Robotics that a cluster of schools can actually afford.

    Robotics

    SELution did not invent these. It packages proven low-cost play methods into one training pathway and one record. Six Bricks is an established DUPLO-based approach; SELution’s activities draw on it and adapt it to each national curriculum.

    In the room

    A session, not a slide

    Real play sessions, and the team that runs them. The whole programme is a box of bricks, a script, and coaching. Nothing here needs power or a procurement cycle.

    How a pilot runs

    A cluster, a term, and a clear before-and-after

    SELution runs inside the existing school system, not as a parallel structure. One pilot is roughly one district cluster for one to two terms.

    1

    Cluster and baseline

    An implementing partner selects 8 to 15 schools in one district. A short play-based assessment gives a foundational-skills and SEL baseline for a sample of classes.

    2

    Train the trainers, then the teachers

    A cascade: SELution trains a small group of national master trainers, who train district facilitators, who run the hands-on teacher training and fortnightly coaching. It is how a programme reaches many classrooms quickly without thinning out.

    3

    Run the strands

    Teachers run 3 to 4 short sessions a week using the scripts and low-cost materials. Robotics kits rotate between schools on a fixed schedule.

    4

    Engage parents

    Weekly take-home play prompts in the local language and a termly showcase where children demonstrate what they built and learned.

    5

    Measure and report

    The per-child records roll up to a cluster dashboard. An endline repeats the baseline. Partner and ministry get one comparable report.

    6

    Decide on scale

    Ministry, partner and funder review the evidence against an agreed threshold and decide whether to extend to the next cluster. The district facilitators stay on as a regional community of practice that carries the next round.

    Materials are deliberately cheap and local: bottle caps, cards, chalk grids, recycled parts, one shared robotics kit per cluster. The expensive input is teacher coaching, and that is where the model concentrates spend.
