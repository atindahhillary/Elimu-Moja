One programme. Four strands. Eight countries that already agreed on the destination.

SELution turns the East African Community’s stated priorities, social and emotional learning, foundational literacy and numeracy, and parental engagement, into something a teacher can run on a Monday morning: structured play across four strands, one training pathway, one measurement spine, ready to pilot across borders.

The opening

The region already reached consensus. It just has not reached the classroom.

The 2nd EAC Regional Education Conference put foundational learning, social and emotional learning, and parental engagement at the centre of the agenda. Across every one of those conversations, the same word kept coming up as the method that works: play. That consensus is real, it is regional, and right now it lives in communiqués. SELution is the delivery layer that carries it into schools.

What the conference said

The commitment

Prioritise foundational learning and SEL, involve parents and communities, and use playful, child-centred pedagogy to get there.

What is missing

The gap

A concrete programme teachers can run, comparable across countries, with the training, the materials and the light-touch measurement already attached.

What SELution adds

The move

Four play-based strands on one pathway, designed for low-resource classrooms, ready to pilot with partners who are already asking for it.

The offer

Four strands, one pathway

Each strand is a set of structured play activities with a clear learning target, a teacher script, and simple materials. They share one training pathway and one progress record, so a school runs them as a single programme rather than four projects.

Strand 01

Social & emotional learning

Naming feelings, turn-taking, calming routines, friendship and conflict repair. The backbone the other three strands lean on.

A session looks like A 20-minute circle game where children pass a “feelings ball”, act out a scenario, and agree one class rule. Teacher script, no printing needed.
Strand 02

Foundational literacy & numeracy

Letter sounds, decoding, number sense and early operations through games, songs, chanting and manipulatives, mapped to the national curriculum.

A session looks like Teams build words or number bonds with bottle-cap tiles against a timer, then teach their answer to another team. Assessment is the game score.
Strand 03

Unplugged coding

Sequencing, loops, debugging and algorithms with no device: floor grids, arrow cards, and children as the “robot” following each other’s instructions.

A session looks like One child writes an arrow-card program, another walks the grid to the target, the class debugs the wrong turns together. Pure logic, zero electricity.
Strand 04

Robotics

The same computational thinking, now with a physical build: low-cost motors, recycled materials and a small kit shared across a cluster of schools.

A session looks like Groups build and program a simple line-follower or a grabber from a shared kit, present it, and pass the kit to the next school on a rota.

Strands 03 and 04 are one continuum: unplugged coding needs nothing, robotics adds a shared kit when a cluster is ready. A school can start SELution with strands 01 and 02 alone.

The method

Play is the spine, not a decoration

Every strand runs on the same play cycle: a clear goal, a game with a rule, a role for every child, a moment of reflection, and a record the teacher keeps in seconds. It is what makes the four strands feel like one programme and what the evidence says actually shifts foundational outcomes.

The record at the end feeds one simple progress view per child, aggregated to class, school and cluster. That is the measurement spine partners and funders see.

The references

Every strand maps to something the region already committed to

SELution is not asking ministries to adopt a new priority. It is offering a way to deliver priorities that are already on the table. The detailed institutional picture, the councils, frameworks and data systems this sits under, is set out in the regional systems brief.

How a pilot runs

A pilot is a cluster, a term, and a clear before-and-after

SELution is designed to be run by an implementing partner inside the existing school system, not as a parallel structure. One pilot is roughly one district cluster for one to two terms.

Cluster and baseline

An implementing partner selects 8 to 15 schools in one district. A short play-based assessment gives a foundational-skills and SEL baseline for a sample of classes.

Train the teachers

Two to three days of hands-on training on the play cycle and the strands the cluster is starting with, then fortnightly coaching visits by the partner.

Run the strands

Teachers run 3 to 4 short sessions a week using the scripts and low-cost materials. Robotics kits rotate between schools on a fixed schedule.

Engage parents

Weekly take-home play prompts in the local language and a termly showcase where children demonstrate what they built and learned.

Measure and report

The 10-second-per-child records roll up to a cluster dashboard. An endline repeats the baseline assessment. Partner and ministry get one comparable report.

Decide on scale

Ministry, partner and funder review the evidence against an agreed threshold and decide whether and how to extend to the next cluster.

Materials are deliberately cheap and local: bottle caps, cards, chalk grids, recycled parts, one shared robotics kit per cluster. The expensive input is teacher coaching, and that is where the model concentrates spend.

Where this is

Early, honest, and already moving

SELution is at the design and partnership stage. What exists today is a defined programme, an implementing partner in conversation, and interested contacts in four countries. What does not exist yet is a funded pilot. That is the ask.
