A regional proposal for the East African Community

From commitments to impact.

The East African Community has the resolutions. Elimu Moja is the machinery that turns them into changes a child in a classroom in Juba, Bujumbura, Kisumu or Mogadishu can feel: a shared scorecard, a common foundational learning framework, a teacher qualification passport, and one regional data warehouse.

One region. Eight flags. One system. Elimu Moja proposes a thin regional layer of shared standards, shared data and shared services across Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, Somalia and DR Congo. The colour band above is drawn from their flags, and runs through this whole page.

The problem

The region keeps agreeing, and staying stuck

East Africa does not lack ambition or evidence. It lacks the shared plumbing that would let a commitment made by ministers travel down to a district officer, a head teacher, a parent, and back up again as data. Everything is standalone.

Gap register

Conference floorLearning

No shared definition of foundational proficiency

Foundational literacy and numeracy was the lead sub-theme, yet each state sets its own bar. Citizen-led tools (Uwezo, PAL Network ICAN and ICAR) and national assessments are not equated.

System response A common minimum-proficiency standard for reading and numeracy in the Foundational Learning Framework, aligned to the Global Proficiency Framework and the Continental Assessment Framework for Africa.

This briefLearning

Foundational learning is not integrated across the region

Social emotional learning, data and evidence, and ICT integration are each addressed somewhere, but no single framework carries them together with a clear roadmap.

System response The EAC Foundational Learning Framework: three pillars, a playful pedagogy spine, a competency crosswalk, and a pre-primary to upper-primary progression.

Advisory review addsLearning

Early childhood is treated as optional

Foundational learning starts before Grade 1. Pre-primary access, financing and workforce are the weakest and least harmonised part of every system. A conference session asked directly whether the region intervenes too late.

System response Pre-primary is the first rung of the progression, with a minimum service definition and a costed pre-primary workforce plan in the scorecard.

Advisory review addsLearning

Language of instruction policy diverges, and there is a book famine

Kiswahili, English, French and Arabic are all languages of instruction across the eight states, with different mother-tongue transition years. Decodable and levelled readers in local languages are scarce. This alone breaks a common learning metric and cross-border mobility.

System response A regional language-of-instruction protocol with shared transition principles, assessment offered in the language of instruction, and a pooled local-language decodable-reader library in the commons.

Advisory review addsLearning

Over-age and out-of-school children are missed

A curriculum-paced, school-based system does not reach the millions who are over-age or out of school. Teaching at the Right Level and accelerated programmes exist but are not a regional entitlement.

System response A regional catch-up and accelerated-learning standard, and household-based assessment so out-of-school children still count.

Conference floorTeachers

Teacher development is episodic and uneven

Sessions repeatedly contrasted one-off training with sustained, school-based support. Quality of continuous professional development varies widely by state and by district.

System response A regional teacher competency standard, a tiered professional pathway, and digital communities of practice built into the passport.

This briefTeachers

No recognised "East African teacher"

There is no shared brand, standard or credential that says a teacher is qualified to practise anywhere in the Community, or to serve as a trainer of trainers across borders.

System response The Teacher Qualification Passport: a universal competency assessment leading to a portable, tiered credential.

Advisory review addsTeachers

No teacher mutual recognition agreement

The EAC has concluded mutual recognition agreements for accountants, engineers, architects and veterinary surgeons under the Common Market Protocol. There is none for teachers, the largest regulated profession in the region.

System response A Teachers MRA drafted against the Continental Teacher Qualification Framework, with the passport as its instrument.

Advisory review addsTeachers

Teacher registries cannot talk to each other, and fraud is hard to catch

Eight teacher services, no shared registry, no common way to verify a certificate issued in another state or to detect a fabricated one. Kenya's qualifications authority has flagged more than 10,000 fake certificates. There is also no shared misconduct list, so a teacher barred in one state can re-register next door.

System response A federated teacher registry index, a credential-verification service, and a binding cross-notification alert when any teacher is struck off.

Advisory review addsTeachers

Mobility could drain the poorest districts

A passport that eases movement, without deployment incentives or portable benefits, can pull teachers from rural, arid and refugee-hosting areas that are already chronically understaffed. Kenya and Tanzania train more teachers than they hire while South Sudan and Somalia face acute shortages. Pensions and social security do not follow a teacher across a border.

System response Hard-to-staff service is a passport tier requirement, plus a totalisation arrangement so benefits follow the teacher, and a regional deployment facility that routes surplus to deficit.

This briefData and AI

No single regional data warehouse

Data lives in country silos. There is no one place where a minister, a researcher, a district officer or a citizen can get a comparable regional number when they need it.

System response A federated regional warehouse: states keep custody of their data, a shared semantic layer makes it comparable, three access tiers govern who sees what.

This briefData and AI

Data does not become decisions

Even where data exists it rarely reaches a policy choice, a budget line or a tracked commitment.

System response An indicator to insight to brief to commitment to impact workflow, with an AI assistant that answers in plain language and cites the source indicator.

This briefData and AI

AI is arriving in classrooms with no safety floor

Tools reach schools ahead of infrastructure, teacher capacity, and any shared rule on children's data or age-appropriate design.

System response A regional AI-in-classrooms safeguarding standard built on the UNESCO guidance and the AU Continental AI Strategy, plus a procurement checklist.

Advisory review addsData and AI

Child-data protection is not harmonised

Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda and Tanzania have data protection laws and authorities; South Sudan has none yet; others are weaker. A cross-border store of minors' data needs a common child-data rule and a lawful transfer route.

System response A child-data minimisation standard, aggregation thresholds, and alignment to the EAC cross-border data flows mechanism.

Advisory review addsData and AI

The middle tier has data it cannot use

District and county officers are where reforms stall. They lack dashboards, comparisons and the capacity to act on evidence. Conference evidence called the middle tier the engine of school improvement.

System response A middle-tier dashboard as a first-class product of the warehouse, not an afterthought, plus a data-use capability track.

This briefMobility and equivalency

Cross-border teaching is not practical

The vision is a teacher in one state teaching confidently in another. Today that is blocked by recognition, registration and standards.

System response Passport plus Teachers MRA plus a regional supply-and-exchange platform for placements and secondments.

Advisory review addsMobility and equivalency

No equivalency table for mobile and displaced learners

The region shared an East African Certificate of Education and a common examinations council until the mid-1970s. Today higher education has a credit transfer system and basic education has nothing. A child moving between states, or a refugee from South Sudan or Somalia, is placed by guesswork.

System response A basic-education equivalency matrix by grade and phase, a lightweight examinations-equating function, and a portable learner record.

Advisory review addsMobility and equivalency

School years and exam calendars do not line up

Different academic calendars and national examination windows make joint assessment, teacher exchange and shared resources hard to schedule.

System response A published regional assessment calendar and a phased move toward compatible term structures.

Conference floorEquity and inclusion

Children with disabilities are unseen and unassessed

Multiple sessions showed learners with disabilities excluded from assessment entirely, so they are invisible in the data and in planning.

System response A regional inclusive-assessment toolkit and accommodations standard, and disability-disaggregated indicators in the scorecard.

Advisory review addsEquity and inclusion

Crisis and refugee education is treated as an exception

South Sudan, Somalia and eastern DR Congo face protracted crises, and Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania and Ethiopia host large refugee populations. Somalia has roughly 58 percent of children out of school. Refugee-teacher certification and learning continuity across borders sit outside the steady-state system, and outside the IGAD Djibouti Declaration architecture the same states are already bound by.

System response Crisis contexts are a design case throughout: portable learner records, refugee-teacher certification routes, low-tech delivery in the framework, and explicit interoperability with the IGAD Djibouti Declaration on Refugee Education.

Advisory review addsEquity and inclusion

Gender parity is measured, gender outcomes are not

Enrolment parity can hide gaps in completion, safety and results. The conference pushed for gender-transformative approaches with accountability, not slogans.

System response Outcome and safety indicators disaggregated by sex, with a comply-or-explain line in the scorecard.

This briefEquity and inclusion

Parents, learners and communities are not really engaged

Engagement stops at the school gate. The Ubuntu principle, I am because we are, is stated but not operationalised in how the system talks to families.

System response A stakeholder engagement model with a defined information path down to parents and community organisations, and learner and parent voice in the scorecard.

This briefSystem and financing

Information does not flow after the conference

There is no structured route from the Secretariat and ministers, through government officials, to non-governmental and community-based organisations working in education.

System response A published dissemination architecture and a live communiqué tracker: every resolution has an owner, a milestone and a status.

This briefSystem and financing

Learning resources and research are not shared

Textbooks, teaching materials and research stay national. Good practice does not travel.

System response An open resource and research commons with a regional licence, curation, and a translation pool.

Advisory review addsSystem and financing

No funding model for the regional system itself

Who pays for the platform, the assessment and the passport? Without a shared financing mechanism the whole thing is donor-dependent and fragile.

System response A blended model: partner-state contributions on a GDP-weighted formula, a pooled donor window, and cost-recovery on premium services.

Advisory review addsSystem and financing

Non-state schools are outside regional quality assurance

Private, faith-based and low-fee schools teach a large and growing share of children, largely outside regional standards and data.

System response Registration and minimum-standard hooks that apply to any provider drawing on the commons or issuing recognised results.

Advisory review addsSystem and financing

Commitments have no teeth

The Community works by consensus. Resolutions carry no compliance mechanism, which is the meta-gap the conference theme itself names.

System response A peer-review and comply-or-explain instrument owned by the Regional Forum on Education, reporting to the Sectoral Council.

Advisory review addsSystem and financing

One timeline for eight very different states will fail

Almost every existing instrument was built for the original Anglophone members. DR Congo is Francophone with a different structure; Somalia teaches in Somali and Arabic amid active conflict; South Sudan and eastern DRC are fragile. Lowest-common-denominator consensus produces nothing.

System response Variable geometry: a coalition of the willing starts, for example Kenya, Rwanda and Uganda, with differentiated timelines, targeted support and open accession clauses for the rest.

Advisory review addsSystem and financing

Climate shocks close schools with no regional response

Floods, drought and heat interrupt schooling across the region. Disaster-risk financing for education has been piloted nationally but is not regional.

System response A shared education continuity protocol and a regional disaster-risk financing facility line in the roadmap.

No gaps match this combination.

The proposal

The system at a glance

Elimu Moja is not a new institution competing with ministries. It is a thin regional layer of shared standards, shared data and shared services that sits under the Sectoral Council and the Regional Forum on Education, and reports upward to the continental strategy.

The eight blocks

Block 01

Regional Scorecard

One agreed indicator set across access, learning, teachers, inclusion, digital and financing. A shared yardstick for the Council.

Block 02

Foundational Learning Framework

Common minimum proficiency, a playful pedagogy spine, a competency crosswalk, and a pre-primary to upper-primary roadmap.

Block 03

Teacher Qualification Passport

A universal competency assessment, a tiered portable credential, and micro-credentials, backed by a Teachers MRA.

Block 04

Regional Data Warehouse

A federated store with a shared semantic layer, three access tiers, and a plain-language AI assistant on top.

Block 05

Large-scale Competency Assessment

A periodic regional measurement of reading, numeracy and life skills, equated to global metrics and household-based so no child is invisible.

Block 06

Community of Practice and Commons

Teacher circles by phase and subject, an open resource and research repository, and structured regional exchanges.

Block 07

Stakeholder Engagement

A defined information path from the Secretariat to parents and community organisations, and a live communiqué tracker.

Block 08

AI Safety and Governance

A safeguarding standard for AI in classrooms, a procurement checklist, and AI competency expectations for teachers and learners.

The layers, in detail

  1. ContinentalAfrican Union Agenda 2063 · Continental Education Strategy for Africa 2026 to 2035 · Continental foundational learning monitoring framework and FLN scorecard · Continental Assessment Framework for Africa · Continental Teacher Qualification Framework · Addis and Global recognition conventions. Elimu Moja aligns and reports up to these.
  2. Regional politicalEAC Treaty · Common Market Protocol Article 11 · Sectoral Council on Education, Science, Technology, Culture and Sports · Regional Forum on Education. These bodies hold the mandate and adopt the standards.
  3. Elimu MojaThe eight blocks: shared standards, shared data, shared services. Owned regionally, governed jointly, run lean. Drawn to scale it is about 7 percent of the institutional stack, thin by design. Mandate and standards flow down into it; evidence and reporting flow back up out of it.
  4. NationalMinistries of education · curriculum institutes · teacher services · examination bodies · national EMIS. Custody and delivery stay here; Elimu Moja does not replace them.
  5. Sub-nationalRegions, counties and districts · the middle tier · schools · teachers · parents and community organisations. Where impact is felt, and where the evidence starts its journey back up.

Principle of subsidiarity: nothing is done regionally that a state can do well alone. The region does the things that only make sense together, comparability, recognition, shared infrastructure and collective bargaining power with partners.

Block 02 · Prototype

EAC Foundational Learning Framework

A prototype for the framework the brief asks for. It is built on three pillars, carried by a playful and low-tech pedagogy spine, mapped to the competencies each state already teaches, and sequenced from pre-primary to upper primary.

Three pillars

P1 Social and emotional learning

Self-awareness, self-regulation, collaboration, empathy and respect, taught explicitly and woven through every subject. Contextualised for East Africa using the ALiVE constructs and a shared SEL measure, not imported wholesale.

P2 Data and evidence

Every classroom, school and district works from current learning data. Teachers assess continuously and group children by need. The framework defines the minimum data a teacher, a head and a middle-tier officer should see, and how often.

P3 ICT integration

Technology serves foundational learning, starting from where infrastructure actually is. QR-coded texts, radio and audio, offline content, oral-reading tools, and teacher AI diagnostics, with a clear rule that no device is a precondition for the core method.

The pedagogy spine: learning through play

Foundational learning should be joyful and hands-on. The framework adopts the five characteristics of playful learning and two concrete, low-cost methods that already work in the region.

Five characteristics

Playful learning is

  • Joyful
  • Meaningful, connected to a child's world
  • Actively engaging, minds-on
  • Iterative, try, test, revise
  • Socially interactive
Method

Six Bricks

Six coloured bricks per child, used for short daily routines that build memory, sequencing, language, number sense and fine-motor control. Proven in South African foundation phase and adapted across Ethiopia and Uganda through PlayMatters.

Method

Unplugged and block coding

Computational thinking without a computer first: sequencing games, algorithms with bottle tops and chalk, then block coding where devices exist. Builds the "learning to learn" and problem-solving competencies from Grade 1.

Competency crosswalk

Kenya's seven Competency Based Education competencies are used as the reference spine, cross-walked to what Uganda, Tanzania and Rwanda already teach, so the framework is pan-regional rather than one country's model exported.

Learning progression and roadmap

Rung 1

Pre-primary, ages 4 to 5

Oral language, phonological awareness, number sense to 10, executive function through play. Minimum service definition, a trained pre-primary workforce, and a school-readiness check using IDELA or MELQO.

Rung 2

Lower primary, Grades 1 to 3

Decoding to fluency in the language of instruction, addition and subtraction fluency, SEL routines, Six Bricks daily. Regional minimum standard assessed at end of Grade 3.

Rung 3

Upper primary, Grades 4 to 6

Reading to learn, multiplicative reasoning and fractions, structured group work, unplugged then block coding, first data-handling.

Cross-cutting

Catch-up track

Teaching at the Right Level style grouping for over-age and out-of-school children, as a regional entitlement not a project.

Assessment stack

Reading and numeracy

ICAN and ICAR

Citizen-led, one-to-one, household-based, aligned to the Global Proficiency Framework and SDG 4.1.1. Reaches children in and out of school.

Life skills and values

ALiVE and a shared SEL measure

Scenario-based tasks contextualised for East Africa, covering problem solving, collaboration, self-awareness and respect.

Classroom

Continuous teacher assessment

Simple, frequent, used to group children by need and to feed the data and evidence pillar. Structured but not fully scripted lesson support, with coaching. Standard formats shared through the commons.

Regional mandate

How a teacher earns it

Step 1Universal assessment against the regional teacher competency standard: subject knowledge, pedagogy, FLN methods, SEL, inclusion, ethics.
Step 2Recognition of prior learning maps existing national qualifications and experience onto the tiers, so no one starts from zero.
Step 3Passport issued as a verifiable digital credential in the data warehouse, with tier, phase and micro-credentials.
Step 4Continuous professional development in a community of practice adds micro-credentials and renews the passport.

What it is built on

  • The African Union Continental Teacher Qualification Framework, the continental standards and competences for the teaching profession, and the Continental Teacher Mobility Protocol, so it is continental-aligned from day one.
  • The African Continental Qualifications Framework and the East African credit and qualifications work, for levelling, recognition of prior learning and micro-credential portability.
  • A new Teachers mutual recognition agreement under Common Market Protocol Article 11, modelled on the existing agreements for engineers, accountants, architects and veterinary surgeons, paired with a totalisation arrangement so pension and social-security rights follow the teacher.
  • A federated teacher registry index so any state can verify a passport instantly, detect a fabricated credential, and receive an alert when a teacher is struck off anywhere in the Community.
A teacher in Somalia can prepare for the regional assessment through the community of practice, earn a Tier 3 passport, and be recognised to train lower-primary teachers in Kenya for a two-year secondment, counting toward a hard-to-staff service credential, without surrendering their national post. Blocks 04 and 08

Regional data warehouse and AI assistant

One warehouse, not one database. Partner states keep custody and control of their data. A shared semantic layer makes it comparable. An assistant on top answers questions in plain language and always cites the source indicator. And a safeguarding standard governs any AI that reaches a classroom.

Federated architecture

A data space, not a central database, in the spirit of the EAC Single Customs Territory applied to data. What is shared regionally is the meaning, not the raw records.

AccessPublic open-data portal · middle-tier dashboards · minister and Forum views · accredited-researcher microdata enclave · plain-language AI assistant
Semantic layerRegional indicator dictionary · common definitions, units and disaggregations · quality flags · alignment to SDG 4, the UNESCO Institute for Statistics and the AU frameworks · speaks SDMX, so it interoperates with global statistics tools
ExchangeAn information mediator between national nodes · standard data contracts and pipelines · SDMX and open metadata · scheduled, validated submission from each national system
CustodyNational EMIS (KEMIS, SDMS, BEMIS, SSEMIS, OpenEMIS, DHIS2) · exam bodies · teacher registries · finance data. Person-level data stays home. Only agreed, mostly aggregated, de-identified extracts move.

Three access tiers

Tier A · Open

Aggregated regional and national indicators

Public, downloadable, versioned. This is the default. Anyone can access it anytime, which is the point.

Tier B · Restricted

Sub-national and school-level data

For officials and accredited researchers under a data-sharing agreement. Access logged. Small cells suppressed.

Tier C · Sensitive

Individual learner and teacher records

Never leaves the custodian state. A pseudonymous, per-state learner key (a salted hash of the national ID or birth record) enables a coherent record across borders without exposing identity. Cross-border use only as verified yes-or-no checks. Child-data minimisation applies.

prototype

AI safety and governance for classrooms

Whatever the infrastructure, a learner should be safe from the start. A regional safeguarding standard, built on the UNESCO guidance for generative AI in education and the African Union Continental AI Strategy, that any tool must meet to be used with children in the Community.

Principle

Human oversight

A teacher is always in the loop. AI advises, it does not decide on a child's placement, grades or discipline.

Principle

Child-data minimisation

Collect the least, keep it the shortest time, process it in-region. No profiling of children for commercial use. Age-appropriate design, following the UNICEF children's data principles and Kenya's child online protection guidelines.

Principle

Equity of access

No tool may become a precondition for core learning. Offline and low-bandwidth paths are required, not optional.

Principle

Evidence before scale

Local evidence of benefit and no harm before any regional endorsement or procurement.

Principle

Teacher capability

AI competency for teachers and learners follows the UNESCO frameworks and is part of the passport and the curriculum.

Principle

Procurement checklist

Data rights, interoperability, exit terms, accessibility and safeguarding clauses that every ministry can attach to a contract.

Blocks 06 and 07

Community, resources and the beyond-conference model

The strongest gap the conference named about itself: what happens between conferences. This block keeps teachers learning together, keeps resources and research moving, and keeps every stakeholder, down to parents and community organisations, in the loop. Ubuntu, made operational.

Community of practice

Teacher circles by phase and subject

Regional, facilitated circles where teachers plan, observe and problem-solve together, online and in clusters. Modelled on the RELI peer-learning approach and the digital communities of practice already piloted in Rwanda. Participation earns passport micro-credentials.

Resource and research commons

One open library for the region

Textbooks, lesson banks, assessment formats, and research, under a common regional open licence. Curated, quality-flagged, and served with a translation pool across Kiswahili, English, French and Arabic. Not limited to books: datasets and evaluations too.

Regional exchanges

Beyond the conference

Structured teacher and official secondments, school twinning, middle-tier study visits, and a standing evidence-to-policy forum, so exchange is continuous rather than a once-a-year event.

Common knowledge base

One place to look things up

The standards, the framework, the indicator dictionary, the roadmap for foundational learning, and the commitment tracker, all in one navigable, versioned reference. This page is a sketch of it.

The information path

A defined route so a decision does not stop at the ministry door. Each step has a named owner, a format and a service standard for how fast it must move.

Level 1Secretariat and Sectoral Council agree a resolution and publish it to the tracker within days.
Level 2Ministries translate it into a national action with a budget line and a focal point.
Level 3Middle tier receives a plain brief, a dashboard and a checklist, not just a circular.
Level 4Schools and teachers get the change in their language, with the materials to act.
Level 5Parents, communities, NGOs and CBOs are told what changed, what to expect and how to give feedback.
ReturnFeedback and data flow back up the same path into the scorecard and the tracker.

Making it real

Governance, and the road from commitments to impact

Run lean, governed jointly, owned by the region. A small delivery capability, clear decision rights, and a phased plan with quick wins in year one.

Who runs it

Direction

Sectoral Council on Education

Sets policy, adopts standards, holds the mandate. The Council of Ministers ratifies instruments such as the Teachers MRA.

Stewardship

Regional Forum on Education

The multi-stakeholder body from the first conference. Owns the roadmap, the peer-review and the comply-or-explain process, convenes partners.

Delivery

A small delivery unit in the Secretariat

Ten to fifteen people. Runs the platform and the standards process, coordinates the assessment, reports to the Forum. Not a new agency.

Custody

National institutions

Ministries, curriculum institutes, teacher services and exam bodies keep delivery and data custody. The region does not replace them.

Technical partners

Existing regional bodies

IUCEA, EASTECO, ADEA, the assessment networks, UNESCO, UNICEF, GIZ and GPE plug in against defined roles rather than parallel projects.

Assurance

Independent evidence function

Networked evidence labs and universities get microdata access and publish an annual independent read of the scorecard.

Phased roadmap

Phase 1

Foundation

0 to 12 months
  • Name a coalition of the willing to move first, with accession clauses for the rest
  • Adopt the indicator framework and publish the scorecard v1 from existing data
  • Stand up the communiqué tracker with every 2026 resolution loaded
  • Finalise the Foundational Learning Framework and the regional minimum proficiency standard
  • Connect the first three EMIS to the warehouse; publish the open tier
  • Draft the Teachers MRA and the teacher competency standard
Phase 2

Build

12 to 30 months
  • Run the first regional foundational and life-skills assessment
  • Pilot the Teacher Qualification Passport in three states, Tiers 1 and 2
  • Launch the community of practice and the resource commons
  • Ship the AI assistant on the open tier and the AI safeguarding standard
  • All eight states connected to the warehouse; middle-tier dashboards live
Phase 3

Scale

30 to 60 months
  • Teachers MRA in force; passport recognised region-wide; Tiers 3 and 4 running
  • Second assessment cycle; scorecard drives an annual Council review with comply-or-explain
  • Catch-up entitlement and inclusive-assessment toolkit adopted regionally
  • Sustainable financing formula agreed; delivery unit funded from partner-state contributions

Quick wins in year one

  • Scorecard v1 and the tracker, from data that already exists, give the Council something to govern with immediately.
  • The Foundational Learning Framework can be published and piloted without waiting for the platform.
  • The community of practice can start with existing networks and cost very little.
  • A verification-only teacher registry index delivers value before the full MRA is ratified.

Risks and mitigations

RiskMitigation
Sovereignty concerns about a regional data storeFederation by design: data stays home, only agreed aggregates move, custody never transfers, each state can audit access.
Consensus culture means no enforcementComply-or-explain and public peer review owned by the Forum; the scorecard makes slippage visible.
Donor dependence and fragmentationPartner-state contribution formula from the start; a single pooled donor window against one roadmap.
Mobility drains the poorest districtsHard-to-staff service required for the higher passport tiers; deployment incentives tied to recognition.
Capacity gaps in fragile and crisis statesDifferentiated timelines, targeted support, crisis contexts treated as a core design case not an exception.
Yet another platform nobody usesBuild for the middle tier first; start from data that exists; ruthless focus on the eight blocks only.

What success looks like by 2030

  • One number the Council trusts for regional foundational learning, moving in the right direction.
  • A teacher qualified in one state can be recognised to teach or train in another within weeks, not years.
  • Any citizen can see comparable regional education data on demand.
  • Every conference resolution has a visible owner and status, and most are delivered.
  • AI reaches classrooms behind a safety floor the region set on purpose.

About this prototype

Elimu Moja is a concept prototype prepared as an advisory response after the 2nd EAC Regional Education Conference, Nairobi, August 2026. It is a proposal for discussion, not an official EAC document, and the name is a placeholder. All country figures, scorecard values, tracker entries and assistant answers are illustrative and synthetic, included to show how the system would work. Framework content is drawn from the public sources below and would need validation with each partner state, curriculum institute and teacher service.

It was informed by a three-track advisory review covering regional integration and teacher qualifications, foundational learning and assessment, and education data systems and AI governance.
